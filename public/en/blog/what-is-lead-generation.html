B2B lead generation: from first contact to a qualified opportunity

Understand contacts, prospects, MQLs and SQLs, then build a measurable path from research and outreach to qualified sales opportunities.

What B2B lead generation means

B2B lead generation is the work of finding relevant organisations, opening a conversation and identifying a commercial reason to continue. A spreadsheet of company names is a research asset. It becomes a useful sales input when the people, need and next step are understood.

The aim is to build a flow your team can work with. More contacts alone do not tell you whether you have better opportunities, enough sales capacity or a profitable acquisition process.

Contacts, prospects and qualified leads

StageWhat it tells youWhat happens next
ContactA record exists for a person or organisation.Check accuracy, relevance and the appropriate way to contact them.
ProspectThe organisation fits your target profile.Research its situation and open a relevant conversation.
MQLMarketing has identified interest under agreed criteria.Review the interest and decide whether a sales conversation is appropriate.
SQLSales has confirmed enough commercial context to proceed.Agree an owner, a next step and a follow-up date.

Teams use MQL and SQL differently. Write down your own definitions with sales before measuring them. A content download or a booked call is a signal to assess, rather than automatic proof of buying intent.

Build the process in five steps

  1. Define your ideal customer profile. Specify sectors, geography, company size, relevant roles and the problem you solve. Also write down who is outside the target.
  2. Choose the route to a conversation. Combine account research and appropriate outreach with useful content, search, events, referrals or paid campaigns.
  3. Qualify the opportunity. Understand the need, stakeholders, timing and commercial fit. Record missing information rather than filling it with assumptions.
  4. Follow up with a reason. Send relevant information, answer questions and agree when to reconnect. Stop when the person declines or asks not to be contacted.
  5. Hand over with context. The sales owner should receive the account history, the reason for interest, qualification notes and the agreed next step.

Choose channels for their role in the buying process

  • Outbound email and LinkedIn: useful for reaching a defined group of organisations, subject to applicable contact rules and platform requirements.
  • SEO and editorial content: help buyers understand a problem and evaluate a supplier over time.
  • Paid search: can reach existing demand, provided the offer, landing page and lead quality are measured together.
  • Paid social: can distribute useful content or specific offers to relevant audiences; a form submission still needs qualification.
  • Events and webinars: create an opportunity for substantive conversations when you prepare the follow-up.
  • Referrals: can provide an introduction with useful context; record where the opportunity came from.

Measure the complete economics

Track spend, relevant replies, qualified conversations, meetings held, opportunities created and closed sales. Compare channels on a consistent definition of quality and over a period that reflects your sales cycle.

Cost per qualified opportunity is campaign cost divided by the number of opportunities that meet your agreed definition. Customer acquisition cost also includes the sales and operational work needed to win the customer. Pipeline value is the value of potential deals; it is not revenue.

For example, if an illustrative campaign costs €10,000 and creates 20 qualified opportunities, its cost per opportunity is €500. That calculation alone says nothing about profit: you still need closed sales, gross margin and the cost of serving the customer.

Tools support the process

Start with a CRM, a clear account list and an owner for every active opportunity. Research and enrichment tools can add context, automation can move information between systems, and reporting can expose missed follow-ups. Additional software is useful only when it solves a bottleneck the team has identified.

  • Buying large lists before defining the target.
  • Measuring downloads and opens as if they were sales.
  • Handing over contacts without context.
  • Leaving follow-up ownership unclear.
  • Expanding volume before checking quality and delivery capacity.

Frequently asked questions

How many leads do we need to win a customer?

Use your own conversion history. Work backwards from a sales target through opportunity, meeting and qualified-lead conversion; there is no reliable universal ratio.

When should we use an agency?

When research, outreach or follow-through need specialist capacity. Agree the scope, qualification criteria, reporting and who owns each stage before you start.

When will results appear?

Research, infrastructure and approvals come first. Early conversations and closed sales happen on different timelines; set review points around your market and sales cycle.
