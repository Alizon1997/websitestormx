Tailored software for SMEs: when convenient Really.

As you decide between custom package management and software, how realistic it costs, and the lock-in vendor that no one explains before signing.

Published on 2026-08-19

TL;DR — A tailor-made software for SMEs makes sense when your process does not enter into any package management, or when you hold the operation together with Excel, email and 3-4 different tools. It costs more at the beginning — in Italy in 2026 it starts from about 5.000€ for a simple form and rises beyond 40,000€ for complex systems — but in the course of 2-4 years the total cost of ownership often falls under that of a standard recurrent license package. The accelerated development with AI compresses the time of the first tool to weeks instead of months, but does not turn a complete ERP into a project for two weeks: distrust of those who promise you.

Tailored software for SMEs: Does it agree or not? It is the question we hear every time an entrepreneur notices that the management used for years is no longer enough — too many parallel Excel sheets, a CRM that does not speak with billing, a process built in the years that no ready software really replicates.

The short answer is that it depends on how much your process differs from the standard. If your way of working is quite common, a ready package costs less and puts you operational first. If instead part of your competitive advantage is the way you work — and adapting to a generic tool means slowing down instead of speeding — the calculation changes.

In this guide we see how you decide, how realistically it costs in 2026, the risk of selling lock-in that almost no supplier explains you before signing, and why accelerated development with AI (what we do with Claude Code) changes the times but not all the rules of the game.

Tailored management vs package software: how you really decide

Package software has an obvious advantage: it costs less at the beginning and is ready immediately. The limit emerges when your processes do not coincide with those provided by the software — you end up adapting the company to the tool, instead of the contrary. A tailor-made software makes the opposite: it is designed on your real processes, not on a medium flow designed for the mass market. Here is the honest comparison, criterion by criterion.

CriteriaPackage SoftwareTailored software
Initial costLow, monthly or annual licenseHigher, one-time investment

Total cost to 3-4 yearsGrows with additional users and modulesOften less than after 2-4 years of use
Dependence on the supplierHigh — data and logic remain in their systemLow, if code and data remain yours
When it makes senseStandard process, limited initial budgetProcess with exceptions, fragmented stack

According to the Digital Innovation Observatory in SMEs of the Politecnico di Milano (report 2025-2026), 76% of Italian SMEs have neither invested nor planned to invest in artificial intelligence, and 47% have not carried out any research and development activities in the last three years. It is not (almost never) bad will: often it lacks time to stop and put a price at the cost of continuing to work as you have always done. If you recognize yourself in the right column of the table above — process with exceptions, fragmented stack — is the signal that is worth making that account.

How much does a tailor-made software cost for an SME (real numbers)

There is no honest list price for "tailored software" — it depends on perimeter, integrations and number of users. But the price guides of custom development suppliers in Italy converge quite consistently on the same orders of magnitude in 2026, and it is worth knowing them before asking for a quote.

LevelWhat typically includesPrice range (Italy, 2026)
SimpleCustomer management, invoices, deadlines5.000 – 15.000 €
AverageDashboard, reporting, integrations between 2-3 tools15.000 – 40.000 €
ComplexMulti-module ERP, multi-sede, mobile app, advanced automation40.000+ €

These amounts should be added a yearly maintenance, typically 15-20% of the initial cost — covers updates, small changes and support. It is the cost that is often underestimated when comparing estimates: a more economical project at the beginning but without a clear maintenance plan costs more in the year two.

The right comparison is not "spoke software vs zero expense": it is custom software vs the real cost of continuing without. Hours lost to copying data between systems, invoicing errors, commercial opportunities that escape because no one has updated CRM in time. That cost rarely ends in an Excel sheet, but it exists.

The lock-in vendor: the risk that no one explains before signing

Vendor lock-in means that, even wanting to change supplier, the cost — economical, operational, organizational — to exit is so high that remains blocked. With a package software it is almost always so for construction: your data live in their format, inside their infrastructure, often without public API documented. Changing platform is not a simple replacement: it requires migrating data, rethinking operational flows and reforming the team on new tools, with weeks of discontinuity weighing on turnover and customer service.

With a custom built software well, the lock-in vendor is not automatic — it depends on who builds it to you. If the code and data remain the property of your company, with standard formats and documentation, you can change who keeps it without starting from scratch. It is the reason why, in our custom software service, the code remains of the customer and there is no contract that keeps you tied to us to use it.

Why accelerated development with AI changes times (and what does not change)

If you've already read some custom software guides, you've probably encountered a warning like this: it's difficult for anyone who promises you a complete system in two weeks. It is a right advice — for a multi-module ERP with multiple integrations, two weeks are unrealistic with any method of development, including AI.

What accelerated development with AI really changes is the first useful perimeter: a targeted tool, a single flow, an internal dashboard. With traditional development, even only this first piece often takes months between planning, revisions and development cycles. When NOT a custom software

For honesty, it's not always the right choice. These are the cases where, when asked, we recommend not to do so — at least not immediately.

  • A SaaS from a few dozen or hundreds of euros per month already covers 90% of the need, and the remaining 10% is not the part of the process where you have a competitive advantage to defend. In that case the custom is a cost, not an investment.
  • The process you want to digitize is not stable yet. If you do this today in different ways from person to person, building software on a chaotic process only produces faster chaos. First you map and simplify, then automate — it is worth the custom software as much as it is worth for AI automation more generally.
  • In the company there is no one who will own the system after the launch. It serves an internal owner that signals problems, asks for changes and decides the priorities of evolution. Without this figure, even the best custom software is degraded in a few months.
  • The initial budget does not even cover the first essential module. Better to start with a ready package and return to the custom when the process — and budget — are more mature.

From where to start: the framework in 3 moves

If you are evaluating a custom software, this is the order we use with our customers — in the same order, not inverted steps:

  1. Map where the process breaks today. Where do you pass from Excel to email to WhatsApp to complete a single operation? Where do you lose more hours, where do you make more mistakes?
  2. Isolate A high friction module, not the whole system. Almost always is what pain is more concrete and easier to measure — not the most ambitious project of the list.
  3. Measure impact on a real business number — spared hours, avoided errors, time of evasion of a practice — before expanding to other modules.

If the flow works on these criteria for a few weeks, it expands to the next module. If it doesn't work, it fixes there, without already betting on a whole system. It is the same principle behind our multichannel outbound system: build, measure, then scale — not the contrary.

FAQ: tailor-made software for SMEs

How much does a tailor-made management software cost for an SME?

In Italy in 2026 a simple form (customer management, invoices, deadlines) starts from about 5.000-15.000 euros. An average system with dashboards, reporting and integrations between 2-3 tools ranges from 15,000 to 40,000 euros. A complex system — multi-module ERP, multi-sede, mobile app, advanced automation — starts from 40,000 euros up. This should be added a yearly maintenance typically equal to 15-20% of the initial cost.

Is it a custom-made management or a package?

Depends on how much your process is disconnected from the standard. If your way of working is quite common, a ready package costs less and puts you operational first. If your process has exceptions that no generic tool handles well, or if you today hold the operation together with Excel, email and 3-4 different software, a custom system often has a total cost of possession less than 2-4 years, even if it costs more at the beginning.

What is the lock-in vendor and how do you avoid in a management software?

The lock-in vendor is when, while wanting to change supplier, the economic and operating cost to exit is so high that it remains blocked: data in their format, logic closed in their infrastructure. It avoids ensuring that the code and data remain the property of the company, with standard formats and documented APIs, not only use licenses on a proprietary system that you can't take away.

How long does it take to develop a custom software?

With traditional development, a complete system typically takes several months. With accelerated development by AI well guided, the first targeted tool — a flow, a module, a dashboard — is often delivered in about 14 days instead of the 6 months typical of a traditional custom project. This is true for the first useful perimeter, not for a complete multi-module ERP: it is difficult for those who promise a whole complex system in two weeks, with any method of development.

Customized software requires an internal IT team to be maintained?

Not necessarily a team, but it serves an internal owner: someone who signals problems, asks changes and decides the priorities of evolution. Without this figure, even the best custom software is degraded over time, because no one updates it when changing processes. Technical maintenance can remain externalized to the supplier that built it, provided the code remains owned by the company.
