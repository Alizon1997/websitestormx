AI receptionists for professional practices: scope, cost and human handover

Use a voice assistant for routine calls and appointment handling while keeping sensitive requests and professional judgement with people.

At a glance. Give the assistant a narrow operational role, connect it to reliable availability and make handover to people clear from the start.

Define the assistant’s role

An AI receptionist can answer routine calls, explain approved office information, collect a callback request and handle specified appointment actions. It should have a clear boundary around professional advice and sensitive decisions.

Medical, dental, legal and accounting practices have different operating and information requirements. Build from the practice’s real call types rather than a generic script.

Suitable tasks and handover points

TaskPossible scopeBoundary
Opening times and directionsUse approved, current office information.Do not invent availability or change policies.
Booking and reschedulingRead authorised slots and apply booking rules.Escalate conflicts or exceptions.
Callback requestsCollect only the details needed for the callback.Avoid unnecessary sensitive information.
Urgent, distressed or complex callersFollow the agreed transfer or escalation procedure.Do not improvise clinical, legal or financial advice.

Identify the responsible person for each escalation, including outside normal hours. An advertised handover route must be available in practice.

Connect to the real calendar

A useful receptionist checks actual availability and confirms that the booking has been written successfully. Define appointment types, durations, preparation instructions, cancellations and who can change a record.

Handle time zones, duplicate requests, unavailable systems and interruptions explicitly. If the system cannot complete a booking, it should say so and create the agreed callback task.

Be clear about the use of AI

Introduce the assistant clearly and offer a practical way to reach a person. Before production, have the practice’s responsible advisers review the proposed data flows, access, retention, call recording and any sector-specific requirements.

Do not assume that using a particular platform or hosting region resolves those questions. The actual configuration, intended use and supplier arrangements need to be understood.

Calculate the cost from real call volume

Collect call counts, minutes, missed calls, peak times and the main request types. A proposal should separate setup, telephony, AI usage, platform costs, integration and ongoing review.

Compare the tasks covered with the work a person still needs to do. A receptionist also handles relationships, unusual requests and work inside the practice. A lower per-minute charge does not show that the whole role has been replaced.

Run a controlled pilot

  1. Select a limited set of routine calls.
  2. Prepare the knowledge base and escalation rules.
  3. Test varied accents, background noise, interruptions and incomplete details.
  4. Verify actual calendar actions and handovers.
  5. Review errors and caller feedback before expanding.

Measure completed bookings, correct routing, callback completion and unresolved requests. Keep someone responsible for updating office information and reviewing the system after changes.

Frequently asked questions

Can the assistant provide professional advice?

Keep its scope to the tasks agreed with the practice. Professional judgement and sensitive decisions should remain with the appropriate qualified person.

Can it answer outside office hours?

It can be configured to do so, but its actions and escalation route must be realistic for those hours.

Is it suitable for a small practice?

Possibly, if missed calls or routine handling create a real problem. Compare the expected benefit with setup and operating work using your own volumes.
