B2B cold email: targeting, infrastructure and responsible follow-up

Build a relevant account list, authenticate your email and measure real conversations without mistaking send volume for pipeline.

At a glance. Relevance, reliable infrastructure and clear reply ownership work together. Measure qualified conversations and check contact rules before scaling activity.

Start with a reason to contact the account

A useful B2B introduction relates a specific business problem to an organisation that may plausibly face it. Research the account, the role and the offer before preparing a sequence. Avoid manufactured personalisation based on invented events or weak assumptions.

Cold email is not automatically appropriate because the recipient works for a business. Determine the applicable contact rules and suppression process for the market and intended use before launch.

Prepare the sending infrastructure

ControlPurposeImportant limit
SPFIdentifies authorised sending infrastructure for a domain.It is one part of authentication, not a delivery guarantee.
DKIMAdds a signature that receiving systems can verify.Signing does not make irrelevant or unwanted messages acceptable.
DMARCDefines policy and reporting around domain alignment.Deploy and monitor it with your actual mail systems in mind.
Sender and list qualitySupport a recognisable identity and relevant audience.Authentication cannot compensate for poor targeting or complaints.

Use a documented sending setup that protects the business and can be monitored. A separate domain does not provide “total protection” for a brand. Avoid lookalike identities intended to mislead recipients.

Test before increasing volume

Check authentication, delivery behaviour, bounce handling, unsubscribe or stop requests and reply routing. Increase activity carefully based on observed quality and the current requirements of your email provider.

There is no fixed daily mailbox volume or warm-up period that guarantees inbox placement. Automated warm-up activity is not a substitute for legitimate messages, a reliable list and recipient feedback.

Keep the message simple

  1. Context: why this company or role is relevant.
  2. Problem: a concrete issue the recipient may recognise.
  3. Offer: how the service could help, in plain language.
  4. Evidence: a relevant, documented example with its context.
  5. Next step: one clear question, with an easy way to decline.

Write as a person making an introduction. Avoid false urgency, misleading reply prefixes and promises unsupported by the service. A long list of capabilities usually makes the first message harder to assess.

Coordinate replies and follow-up

A real reply should reach a person who can continue the conversation. Update the CRM, pause conflicting sequences and record the next step. A refusal or request to stop should be respected across the relevant workflow.

Do not treat a tracking open as consent or proof of interest. Privacy features, automated scanning and image loading can distort opens. WhatsApp or phone follow-up needs its own appropriate context and channel controls.

Measure quality and commercial progress

  • Delivery failures and complaints.
  • Relevant replies, separated from automatic responses.
  • Qualified conversations and the reasons for rejection.
  • Meetings held and the quality of handover.
  • Opportunities that progress and closed sales.

Avoid diagnosing every weak response rate as a technical issue. A poor target, unclear offer, unsuitable timing or ineffective follow-through can also be responsible. Use your own baseline instead of a promised universal benchmark.

Keep legal assumptions out of campaign shortcuts

A business address, public directory listing or relevance to a job role does not by itself establish that unsolicited promotional email is permitted. Have the responsible compliance owner assess the intended campaign and markets, and document how preferences and stop requests are handled.

Authentication, personalisation and a polite opt-out are useful operational controls. They are not a blanket legal authorisation to contact any B2B recipient.

Frequently asked questions

How many emails should one mailbox send?

Follow the current provider requirements and a monitored operating plan. There is no universal safe number that overrides recipient feedback or acceptable-use rules.

Is a low reply rate always a deliverability problem?

No. Review delivery evidence alongside target fit, the offer, message and follow-up. Opens alone cannot settle the diagnosis.

Can a campaign guarantee appointments?

No. Test a defined target and offer, then assess the actual conversations and sales outcomes.
