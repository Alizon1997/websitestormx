B2B appointment setting: what a qualified meeting should include

Define qualification, attendance and handover before comparing appointment-setting services or cost per meeting.

At a glance. Agree the meeting criteria and the handover. Track booked meetings, held meetings, opportunities and sales as distinct outcomes.

A booked meeting is a specific deliverable

Lead generation can produce relevant contacts or expressions of interest. Appointment setting continues to a proposed time, an accepted invitation and a conversation that meets agreed criteria. The client’s sales team still needs to run the meeting and own what follows unless a broader sales scope has been agreed.

Write down the target company profile, relevant roles, reason for interest and information required before the invitation is sent. Calendar activity without that context is difficult to evaluate.

Agree what counts

  • Target fit: the company, market and role match the brief.
  • Commercial context: the contact understands the topic and has a plausible reason to discuss it.
  • Confirmation: the invitation contains the purpose, participants, time zone and meeting link.
  • Attendance: booked, held, cancelled and rescheduled meetings are reported separately.
  • Handover: the sales owner receives the conversation history, qualification notes and next-step expectations.

An appointment that costs €100 and has a 50% attendance rate costs €200 per meeting held, before sales time. This is an arithmetic example, not a sector benchmark. Compare providers on held-meeting quality and resulting opportunities, rather than the unit price alone.

Attendance is part of the process

Qualify before proposing a slot. Use a clear subject and agenda, check the time zone and provide an easy way to reschedule. Reminders should use the channels the contact has agreed to use. When someone cannot attend, record the reason and decide whether a new invitation is appropriate.

Review no-shows for patterns: weak interest, unclear invitations, long gaps before the meeting or poor handover. Repeatedly sending reminders cannot fix a meeting the prospect never understood or wanted.

Separate early activity from sales results

In the Profood case, the initial work generated 11 confirmed meetings with international buyers in 90 days. The wider engagement reported more than €345,000 in orders across 12 months. These describe different stages and timeframes.

Do not turn that sequence into a promised conversion rate for another client. Product, market, order size, sales effort and seasonality all affect what follows a meeting. Report opportunity progression and closed sales separately from appointment volume.

Choose the right responsibility after the meeting

Appointment setting can be enough when your team has capacity to prepare proposals, answer questions and manage negotiations. If that work has no clear owner, a broader sales-development collaboration may be more useful.

Before launch, agree who prepares, attends, follows up, updates the CRM and approves commercial terms. Storm X can support a wider part of the process within the scope set out with the client.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good show rate?

Use your own baseline and compare like-for-like meetings. Track attendance and reasons for cancellations; a universal percentage cannot tell you whether the meetings are commercially useful.

Should no-shows be replaced?

That is a commercial term to define before starting, together with valid cancellations, duplicate contacts and the treatment of rescheduled meetings.

Can we run appointment setting internally?

Yes, if research, contact handling, qualification and calendar coordination have dedicated owners. Compare the internal capacity with the service scope rather than assuming outsourcing is always better.
