Integrate CRM and Invoicing: the Guide without developers

CRM, invoicing and e-commerce that you speak on your own: the flows that really count, the real costs of Make.com and the mistakes we see most often when an SME tries alone.

Published on 2026-09-02

TL;DR — Integrate CRM and Invoicing means to automatically transfer the data between the software you already use, without double manual insertion. With no-code tools like Make.com you do without developers: the plan suitable for most SMEs costs 9-29€/month, the first 3-5 streams are produced on average in 14 days, and eliminate most repetitive manual work on sales, billing and e-commerce. The cost that really counts is not the software — it is well mapping the exceptions (discounts, different VAT rates, foreign customers) before building, because it is there that the automations made quickly break.

How do you do it? integrate CRM and billing without hiring a developer or remaking the management? It is the question that we hear most often from those who manage the administration of an SME: CRM keeps track of the negotiations, the management issues the invoices, and in between there is a person copying numbers from one screen to another — customer name, amount, VAT rate, payment conditions — every single time a deal closes.

It's not a problem of organizational laziness. According to the Digital Innovation Observatory in the SMEs of the Politecnico di Milano, more than one Italian SMEs out of two (54%) declares to invest with intensity in digital technologies, but only 19% adopts advanced solutions in a structured way — most buy software, but does not make them talk to each other. The result is what we see on almost every management that passes us by hand: good tools, used as separate islands.

In this guide we see what it really means "integrating" CRM and billing, the three streams to automate first, how much it costs to do it with no-code tools like Make.com, and the most common mistakes when an SME tries without initial process mapping.

What does "integrate CRM and invoicing" mean?

Integrating does not mean buying a single mega-management that does everything — for most SMEs is the wrong choice: it costs more, it requires a risky data migration, and often the invoicing module of a CRM "all in one" is weaker than a dedicated electronic billing software. Integrating means keeping the best tools for each function — a good CRM for sales, a good electronic billing software, maybe a dedicated e-commerce — and build the "bridges" that make the data travel automatically between them.

That bridge can be built in three ways, with very different costs and times:

  • By hand — what almost every SME does today: someone reads from one screen and recopys on the other. zero apparent cost, very high real cost in hours and errors.
  • No-code, with Make.com or similar — build the flow with a visual editor, without writing code. It covers the vast majority of real cases of an SME.
  • Custom Development — a custom-made integration in code, via direct API. It makes sense only for very high or logical volumes of business too specific for a no-code tool, and it is the territory of our custom software, not this guide.

For 90% of the SMEs we see, the second option is the right one: predictable cost, no internal technical team needed, and — often underestimated point — if tomorrow changes CRM or management, update a module within the flow instead of rewriting an entire integration.

How much does it cost to integrate CRM and billing (real numbers)

The most common confusion is to think that the cost is the software. It's not. The Make.com plan covering most of the needs of an SME ranges from 9 to 29€/month according to the public list, regardless of how many flows you build on — the scale price with the volume of "operations" performed, not with the number of automations.

The real cost is the design time. In our service Related software, the average setup of the first flows is 14 days, and eliminates most repetitive manual work on those processes. But those 14 days are not "connect two apps" — they are above all map exceptions: special discounts, different VAT rates for Italian and foreign customers, returns, credit notes, cases where a customer appears twice with different emails. A company that skips this stage and tries to build the flow alone, looking at a tutorial, usually does it again after two weeks, when the first case comes that the flow did not foresee.

For comparison, a custom-made code integration — sensible only beyond certain volumes or with very specific logic — starts from figures comparable to a simple software module: the same order of magnitude (5.000-15.000€) that we describe in the guide on tailor-made software for SMEs, more recurring technical maintenance. For most of the three flows seen above, it is a cost that is rarely justified.

Make.com, Zapier or custom development: how to choose

Three different roads, for three different situations:

  • Make.com — the right choice when flows have more than 2-3 steps, conditions (if the VAT rate is different, if the customer is abroad, if the amount exceeds a threshold) or error management. It is the tool we use for almost all integrations CRM, invoicing and e-commerce that we build for customers.
  • Zapier — remains preferable for a simple 1-2-step flow or if you need a niche app that only Zapier integrates natively. The complete operating comparison, with real price numbers, is in our article Make.com vs Zapier.
  • Custom Development — makes sense only when the volume of operations is so high to make no-code plans expensive, or when business logic has too many exceptions for a visual editor. It is a minority case, but real: we talk about it in the guide on tailor-made software for SMEs.

An error we often see is the choice on the contrary: starting with custom development "because it is more solid", when a well-designed no-code stream would solve the same problem in a tenth of time and budget. The custom is justified after, when a no-code flow really becomes the bottleneck — not before.

The most common errors when you connect CRM and billing

Five errors we see coming back, in order of frequency:

  1. No error management. The flow works perfectly until everything goes as planned. The day when the API of the billing software is slow or a mandatory field is missing, automation is blocked silently — and no one notices until the customer asks why he has not received the invoice.
  2. Duplicate clients. Without a clear rule on what identifies a customer (VAT, not name and surname), the same customer ends two or three times in CRM with slightly different data, and subsequent invoices point to the wrong record.
  3. VAT rates not mapped. A flow tested only with Italian customers breaks to the first EU or extra-EU customer, where the rate and tax regime change. It should be provided immediately, not added after the first error in invoice.
  4. The "source" field is lost on the street. If the flow does not take with it from where a lead or an order arrives (campaign, channel, referral), you lose the ability to measure what it works — a problem that you discover only months later, when you need to understand what to invest.
  5. No one controls the flow after launch. Automation is not "finished" when it leaves. Apps change APIs, tariff plans change limits, business processes evolve. Without minimal monitoring (alert on errors, execution logs), a flow that worked in March can silently stop doing so in June.

None of these errors is complicated to avoid — they only require to be expected before constructing the flow, not discovered after. This is why, in our process, the mapping phase comes before the construction of scenarios, not together.

From where to start: the framework in 3 moves

  1. Map where you really lose time. For a week, note each time someone copies data manually between two tools. You do not need software to do it — just a sheet. At the end of the week you will have the 3-5 flows with the highest return, not those that seem more urgent than belly.
  2. Start from the flow with fewer exceptions. Not from the most important — from the simplest to do well. A first flow that works smoothly builds confidence in the process; a first flow that breaks on the third day destroys it.
  3. Document before climbing. Every flow must remain understandable to those who did not build it: if only a person in the company knows how it works, you moved the bottleneck, you did not eliminate it.

FAQ: integrate CRM and billing

How much does it cost to integrate CRM and billing with Make.com?

The software is not the main cost item: the Make.com plan suitable for most SMEs ranges from 9 to 29€/month according to the public list, regardless of how many flows you build. The real cost is the time of planning and testing of flows — in our experience the setup of the first 3-5 flows takes on average 14 days when following it who has already done so, more if you proceed for attempts without an initial mapping of the processes.

Can my CRM connect to the billing software without programming?

In the vast majority of cases yes. Make.com and similar tools connect over 1,000 apps via native integrations, and almost every modern CRM or management exposes an HTTP API that these tools can read even without a dedicated connector. The cases in which it really serves custom development are few: legacy systems without APIs, business logic with too many exceptions, or volumes that exceed what a no-code plan manages in a sustainable way.

What happens if I change CRM or management after automating flows?

If the streams are built well, change an app into a Make.com scenario is a 1-2-hour job, not a reconstruction from scratch — replace the module that connects to the old tool with that of the new, the logic of the flow remains. It is one of the real advantages compared to a custom coded integration, where changing a upstream system often means rewriting part of integration.

Better Make.com or Zapier to connect CRM and billing?

For flows with multiple steps and conditions — typical of CRM and billing, where it serves to manage different VAT rates, foreign customers, or cases of error as a duplicate invoice — Make.com averages 60-80% less than Zapier at equal volume, because invoice for operation and not for task. Zapier remains preferable for a simple 1-2-step flow or if you use a very niche tool that only Zapier integrates natively. The complete comparison is in our article dedicated.

How long does it take to automate order-invoice-contability flow?

On its own, this single stream is typically built in 2-4 working days once the limit cases are clarified (discounts, returns, multiple VAT rates, foreign customers). The part that takes more time is not to connect the two apps, but to map all the exceptions that you manage by hand today without noticing it — is there that almost every automation made quickly breaks to the first real case.

Does CRM automation work with Italian electronic invoicing (SDI)?

Yes: the main electronic billing software used by Italian SMEs (Fatture in Cloud, Aruba, TeamSystem, among others) expose APIs or integrate natively with Make.com, so the switch to SDI remains managed by your billing software — automation only takes care of getting the correct data (customer, amount, rate) from CRM to management, without manual re-insertion.
