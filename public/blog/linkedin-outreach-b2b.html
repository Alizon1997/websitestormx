LinkedIn outreach for B2B: relevant conversations and clear follow-up

Use account research, a credible profile and concise messages to support a coordinated B2B sales process.

At a glance. Research the account, explain the reason for contact and coordinate the next step. Respect channel rules and measure commercial conversations.

Start with a defined account list

LinkedIn is useful when the people involved in a purchase have an active professional presence. Identify the company, the likely buying roles and a specific reason to contact each person. A relevant role is a research clue, rather than proof that the person wants a sales message.

Keep the target narrow enough to understand it. A distributor, an end customer and a potential partner need different conversations even when they work in the same sector.

Make your own profile useful

  • State who you help and the problem you work on.
  • Use a real identity, current company details and a clear service description.
  • Make relevant evidence easy to find without claiming that one client result is typical.
  • Keep the website and contact routes consistent with the message.

Write a message with a reason

A useful introduction explains why this person is relevant, offers one specific point and asks for a small next step. Avoid presenting an entire service catalogue in the connection request.

Illustrative introduction: “I noticed your team is expanding its distributor network in Germany. We support export teams with buyer research and commercial follow-through. Is that something you are currently handling internally?”

Adapt the wording to verified context. Do not pretend to have read a post, met at an event or received a referral when that has not happened.

Coordinate LinkedIn with the wider account history

Keep a distinct plan for the channel rather than copying the email sequence into it. A reply, refusal or request for a later date should update the CRM and stop conflicting follow-ups elsewhere.

A connection acceptance is not a sales qualification. Ask a relevant question, read the response and decide whether a meeting would be useful. Continue only when there is a reason to do so.

Respect platform limits and contact preferences

LinkedIn’s features and restrictions can vary by account and change over time. Check the current platform rules before using tools or scaling activity. No fixed daily invitation number guarantees that an account is safe.

Do not use hidden automation or account rotation as a way to bypass restrictions. Review how contact data is collected and used for your intended market, and record requests to stop. A public profile does not remove that responsibility.

Measure conversations rather than invitations

Track relevant replies, qualified discussions, meetings held and opportunities that progress. Compare the time spent researching and responding with the commercial value of those conversations. Connection acceptance alone is an incomplete success metric.

Sales Navigator may help with account research and filtering. Evaluate it against the research task and current plan capabilities; a subscription cannot compensate for an unclear target or weak offer.

Frequently asked questions

Should LinkedIn and email use identical messages?

No. Keep the account context shared, but write for the channel and the stage of the conversation.

How many follow-ups should we send?

Use a limited, relevant plan and stop on refusal or when the contact asks you to. Review whether each follow-up adds useful information.

Can LinkedIn guarantee a steady flow of appointments?

No channel can guarantee that independently of target fit, offer, execution and the sales process. Test a defined scope and review the quality of outcomes.
